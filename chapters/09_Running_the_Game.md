# 9. Running the Game

This chapter is for the GM. Everything a player needs is in chapters 1–8.

## Your Job
1. **Describe Halcyon** vividly and briefly: smell, sound, one weird detail.
2. **Ask questions** and use the answers. *"Vex, which gang tattooed you?"*
3. **Call for rolls only when failure is interesting.** Otherwise say yes.
4. **Make failures move the story:** fail, succeed at a cost, or things get worse. Never "nothing happens."
5. **Hand out Juice** generously; roughly one per player per session, more for great moments.

## Setting DCs
Pick the DC from the [Core Rules](01_Core_Rules.md#difficulty-dc) table: **13 when unsure.** Add advantage or disadvantage for circumstances instead of fiddling with numbers. If an enemy is opposing the check directly, use its **TN**.

## Running the Enemy Phase
- Declare all enemy actions at once: *"The two Cleaners fire at Vex and Juno; the Drain Beast grabs Ozzie."*
- Players roll their defenses; you just read out Damage and riders.
- Mooks and Soldiers act simply. Elites and Bosses should feel smart: focus the weak, punish clumping, flee when beaten.
- **Spread attacks** unless the fiction says otherwise. Focusing one player every round is how you get a death in a Standard fight.
- Let the fiction override the grid: a Brute can smash a wall, a Skirmisher can jump down a stairwell.
- **Area attacks catch whoever is inside.** Be honest about positions. Heavies (big Area, Engulf) are built to punish a bunched-up party; tell players that spreading out is the counter, and don't line them up in a corridor for the Heavy unless they walked into it.
- **Packs focus.** Pack, Flanker and Overwhelm creatures gang up on one freelancer; that's their whole point. Everyone else should still spread attacks.

## Missions
GRAFT contracts follow a simple shape. Each is about one session.
1. **Brief.** A GRAFT handler explains the job and the pay. Players ask questions, buy gear.
2. **Approach.** Getting in: travel, a social obstacle, or a skill challenge.
3. **Trouble.** One or two fights or hazards. Something is not what the brief said.
4. **Heart.** The objective: a boss, a choice, a sample, a person.
5. **Out.** Escape, consequences, a Breather if earned.
6. **Debrief.** Get paid, level up, downtime.

### The Spill Clock
Give each mission a **6-segment clock**. Tick it when players dawdle, make noise, Overload, or fail a roll badly. When it fills, the situation escalates: reinforcements, a flood, a Cleaner squad, the building collapses. Then reset it. Show it to the players; it makes them hurry.

### Skill Challenges
For a chase, a hack, or a climb, ask for **3 successes before 3 failures** (Standard DC). Each player says how they help and picks a stat. Each failure costs something: HP (T-level Damage), a tick on the Spill Clock, or lost gear.

## Downtime (between missions)
Each freelancer picks **one**:
- **Clinic:** install your new node, splice a Sample, remove a Scar or Fault, or (at levels 4 and 7) go through a supervised **Chrysalis**.
- **Side Gig:** earn 50 × level Scrip.
- **Dig:** learn a secret about the next mission (the GM answers one question truthfully).
- **Rest:** clear all Injuries and gain 1 Juice at the start of next session.
- **Bond:** spend time with a contact; they'll do you a favour on the next mission.

## Evolution at the Table
Evolutions (levels 4 and 7) are the biggest moments in a freelancer's life. Make them land.
- **Seed it.** In the mission before, describe symptoms: an itch under the plating, a second heartbeat, a voice in the wiring, clothes that don't fit.
- **Chrysalis (optional downtime scene).** At the debrief where someone reaches level 4 or 7, play one short scene of the change starting: a cocoon in a flooded basement (Bio), or an emergency overhaul on a clinic table while GRAFT techs argue (Chrome). Ask the player one question: *"What do you lose?"* (a face, a voice, a fear, a friend's trust). Write it down; it's a hook.
- **Emergence.** In the next mission's first fight, the player Emerges at the start of their first turn ([chapter 2A](02A_Forms_and_Evolution.md#emergence-the-moment-it-happens)): temporary HP, nearby enemies Shaken, a free Signature Surge. Give them the spotlight. Describe enemies backing away.
- **Let them win big once.** The first mission after an Evolution should feel like a victory lap. Then raise the stakes (see below).
- **Different speeds.** Everyone Evolves at the same level, so the whole party spikes together. Lean into it: the Spill Clock, GRAFT and the factions all *notice* evolved freelancers.

## Difficulty Across the Campaign
Every enemy has a **Boss Rating** (one of it is a fair fight for 4 level-N freelancers) and a **Minion Rating** (four of it are). Build fights with the unit budget in [Threats](10_Threats.md#5-building-an-encounter): **one unit per freelancer is a Hard (fair) fight**, ×½ Easy, ×¾ Standard, ×1¼ Deadly. The ratings already account for each level, including the Evolutions, so the same budget works from level 1 to 10.

What the Evolutions change is **last stage's monsters**. A creature rated in the previous stage falls apart against freshly Evolved freelancers (four MR 3 creatures: Hard at level 3, Easy at level 4), and one rated in the next stage is twice as dangerous as its number suggests.

| Party level | Plan | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 (Base) | Standard fights, one Hard per mission | Downs happen; Rats and Pickers teach Pack tactics, the Drain Beast and Boiler Hulk teach spreading out |
| **4** (just Evolved) | A victory lap | Bring back level-3 enemies (they're Easy now), then one Hard fight with Evolution I creatures so the new body has something to push against |
| 5–6 | Hard set pieces | Mix a Heavy centrepiece with a Pack; add Purgers and enemies that hit weak defenses (Resist for Sirens, Endure for Oracles) |
| **7** (Evolved again) | A victory lap with bigger monsters | Level-6 enemies are Easy now; open with them, then show a Feral Evolved or a Gutter Leviathan |
| 8–10 | Hard and Deadly fights; the finale should be Deadly | Bosses whose Shift lands after Final Form is spent; Seraph Gunships over open ground |

**Between Breathers:** a fully rested party can take about **one Hard fight plus one Standard fight**, or three Standard fights, before it needs one (an estimate from HP lost per fight; the sims only test fully rested parties). The ratings assume full HP and Flux 0, so a tired party should face less.

**Checks don't scale.** A DC 16 door stays DC 16. At high level, threaten what Forms are *bad* at (ask the Juggernaut for a Gumption check, the Oracle to win an arm-wrestle), and put Extreme (19), Mutant (22), Monstrous (25) and Mythic (28) obstacles in the way. A level 7 primary stat of +8 makes Hard checks routine; that's the point of Evolving.

## Rewards
- **Levels:** one per mission ([Advancement](07_Advancement.md)).
- **Scrip:** 200 + (100 × mission level) each, plus bonuses for style or recovered Samples.
- **Samples:** from Bosses and some Elites.
- **Juice:** in the moment.

## Pacing Guidelines
- **Fights:** 2–3 per mission. One should be Hard. The boss can be Deadly if the players found a way to soften it. Size every fight with the [encounter budget](10_Threats.md#5-building-an-encounter), and scale Bosses for party size ([Threats §6](10_Threats.md#6-party-size)).
- **Breathers:** let the party take them, but make the clock tick.
- **Surges:** encourage them. If a player never Unleashes, ask what their body is doing. Surges are the fun.
- **Overload:** describe it gleefully, never as a punishment. It's the setting coming through the character.

## Safety
Body horror can hit hard. Before play, agree as a table what's off-limits (lines) and what should happen off-screen (veils). Anyone can pause a scene, no questions asked.
